{% comment %} A three-column grid (1fr / auto / 1fr), not flexbox, on purpose: the left block (logo+search) and right block (bell/friends/avatar) are never the same width, so centering the middle nav within "whatever space is left between them" (the old flex-1 + justify-center approach) visibly drags it toward whichever side is narrower. Two equal 1fr columns anchor the middle column at the header's true center regardless of how wide either side's own content is - `justify-self-start`/`-end` then pins each side's content to the correct edge of its own (possibly much wider than its content) column. Each block also gets an explicit col-start-N. Without it, on mobile where the middle
is display:none, CSS grid's auto-placement algorithm (which places items into the next free cell in DOM order) drops the right block straight into column 2 - the display:none nav is excluded from auto-placement entirely, it doesn't just leave column 2 "empty and skipped" - so the icon cluster ends up squeezed next to the logo, with column 3's 1fr share of free space sitting empty and unused past it (exactly the "icons drift back to center, big dead gap on the right" bug this keeps regressing to). Explicit placement isn't subject to that reflow, so it holds regardless of whether the middle item renders. {% endcomment %}
